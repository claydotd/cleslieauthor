## From Drugs to Rivalries 

February was a real mixed bag in terms of themes. 

I started off by reading _Wonder Drug_ by Jennifer Vanderbes. This was a bit of a monster of a book but so interesting to get my teeth into. I really enjoyed how it was written in more of a narrative format and felt that I learned a lot. The story of how thalidomide came to become so widely used and the accompanying scandals and real world harm was definitely a harrowing read, but one I felt good for having learned about. 

For similar reads I would recommend Brian Deer’s _The Doctor who Fooled the World_ which is similarly narrative driven, though a lot more focused on the author’s experience of investigating the individual. 

Next up came _The Group_ by Mary McCarthy. This was a charity shop find about a group of friends navigating growing up in America. Sort of the _Friends_ for the time, except everyone’s rich and went to some sort of fancy college. And it’s the 1930’s. I got a good kick out of it for it’s attitude and the sort of gung ho approach to life the girls all have. It feels like an optimistic read hazed in a naive view of how simple life used to be pre-internet (emphasis on the word naive though, this time had it’s own major issues, like racism, systemic inequality, and so on). But there’s still something very _Famous Five_ -esque about the novel and I enjoyed following the lives of the woman described in it, who were all very different and quite funny. 

The next one is probably the complete opposite of _The Group_ … _Heated Rivalry_ by Rachel Reid was a book I knew I had to read after watching the series. This is exactly what I want in a winter read - something easy to dip into, nothing too important or high stakes is happening, but the characters are likable enough you just want to make sure they have a good time. I thoroughly enjoyed this, and though romance novels aren’t usually my go-to genre I won’t every say no to something new. This was an enjoyable romp that helped get me through the dark winter nights, so thanks Shane and Ilya!

Finally, just managing four this month, I read _The Yellow Wallpaper_ by Charlotte Perkins Gilman. I was surprised that this was the first time I’d dipped into this, considering my absolute favourite genre of book is what I like to call “sad crazy person”. This fit solidly into that category and for that I am grateful. Following the slow descent into madness of the novel I felt such a distinct sense of horror throughout it. It feels modern, but sits solidly with Poe’s work in my mind. Love a gothic horror and this was definitely that. 

Overall quite an eclectic February but one that matched the vibe of the month I think. See you next week for March’s roundup!
